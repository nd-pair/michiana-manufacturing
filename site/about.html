About the data

The map shows {{COMPANIES}} companies at {{SITES}} sites in the South Bend–Elkhart region. Every figure on it comes from a public source, listed below with how it is matched, how current it is, and what it leaves out. Built {{BUILT}}.

Positions

Columns stand at geocoded street addresses (OpenStreetMap Nominatim). SBIR firms stand at the address on their latest award. {{CITYLEVEL}} entries are placed at their city's center instead, because their address is missing or could not be geocoded (OpenStreetMap Nominatim, then the U.S. Census Bureau's geocoder). Several organizations at one address are spread in a small ring so each stays visible. The globe is true scale; county boundaries are from the U.S. Census Bureau and shorelines from Natural Earth. The model of the Main Building is textured with crops of "Golden Dome frontal" by Eccekevin (2016), licensed CC BY-SA 4.0; the crops are mirrored, masked and resized, and are shared under the same license.

Headcounts

Column height is headcount on a log scale. Every figure carries a year and a grade, shown in the roster and the detail panel:

  • Estimate. HIFLD's employee figures are modeled estimates; the year shown is the base dataset's (2022), not a survey date. Business-directory ranges are graded the same way.
  • Reported. Figures a company states itself (website, filing, press release) or reported on an SBIR award, with that year.
  • Company-wide. Marked separately where only a figure for the whole company exists, as for the Elkhart RV manufacturers.

Staff totals in the panels add the known figures as they are, so they are approximate and mix years.

Capabilities, employment and the timeline

Capabilities are inferred from NAICS codes and product keywords with an editable mapping; a capability with one or no company in the current selection is flagged as a gap. Manufacturing employment is from the BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, private ownership, annual averages, {{QCEWYEARS}} (pulled {{QCEWPULLED}}). The sector view sums 3-digit NAICS rows over the region's counties; BLS withholds cells that would reveal a single employer, so sector totals are a lower bound. The workforce panel draws on four BLS programs. Specialization, pay and establishment counts are from the same QCEW files ({{SPECYEAR}}); the location quotient divides an industry's share of the region's private jobs by its share nationally, and region-wide job counts are lower bounds where BLS withholds a county's figure. Occupations are from Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (May {{OEWSYEAR}}) for the South Bend–Mishawaka, Elkhart–Goshen, Niles and Michigan City–La Porte metro areas, which leave out counties such as Kosciusko and Starke; estimates BLS cannot publish are omitted. Monthly manufacturing jobs are from Current Employment Statistics and county unemployment from Local Area Unemployment Statistics, not seasonally adjusted, through {{BLSMONTH}} (pulled {{BLSPULLED}}). The timeline lists {{EVENTS}} dated events: federal awards of $250,000 or more, SBIR/STTR awards and, where available, patents.

Physical-AI Opportunity Index

The index asks where humanoid robots could make the region's manufacturers more competitive: taking on the hard, repetitive and hazardous work so the same or more people can make more complex, higher-mix products. It is not a count of jobs at risk. Six pillars each compare the region with the U.S. average, set to 100, and the index is their geometric mean, so one weak pillar pulls it down.

  • Task fit: local jobs in production, engineering and material-handling occupations (BLS OEWS, May {{OEWSYEAR}}, the four metro areas) weighted by how much of each occupation's work is handling and moving parts, repetitive motion, bending, kneeling and long standing, and hazardous equipment, fumes and heat, from the O*NET 31.0 work-context and work-activity ratings (CC BY 4.0), per job, against the same measure for the U.S.
  • High-mix potential: the share of manufacturing jobs in custom and short-run industries (machine and job shops, fabrication, molds and dies, RVs and trailers, boats, custom woodwork, medical devices; a lower bound where BLS withholds county figures), and workers per establishment, where smaller scores higher (BLS QCEW).
  • Labor pressure: the unemployment rate (lower scores higher), the share of manufacturing workers aged 55 and older, and quarterly manufacturing turnover, from the Census Bureau's Quarterly Workforce Indicators, averaged over the latest four quarters with data.
  • Adoption readiness: industrial machinery mechanics, electricians, engineering and mechatronics technicians, and industrial, mechanical, electrical and software engineers per 1,000 jobs.
  • Local supply: twice the share of a humanoid's cost available from two or more local suppliers, so that half the robot sourced locally scores 100; there is no U.S. benchmark for this pillar.
  • Shared prosperity: growth of manufacturing jobs and average pay over the latest five years against the U.S., and manufacturing value added per job (BEA county GDP over QCEW jobs, for the counties BEA publishes). Because it is part of the index, gains that came with falling jobs or pay would lower it.

Weights are equal, and the weights, traits and lists are editable. The index is saved every month.

Notre Dame research in the same panel lists the federal grants, cooperative agreements and contracts to the University of Notre Dame since FY2016, and its subawards on other institutions' awards, whose title (or description, at least three times) is about robots, autonomy and drones, prosthetics and exoskeletons, haptics, legged or humanoid systems or robotic manufacturing, from USAspending.gov, with investigators for NSF awards from the NSF Award Search. Amounts are obligations so far; industry, foundation and state funding are not included.

Humanoid supply chain

The humanoid panel measures progress toward manufacturing physical AI in the region. It divides a full-size humanoid robot into {{HUMCOMP}} components (motors, gear reducers, screws, bearings, encoders, drivers, the main computer, control boards, harnesses, battery, cameras, inertial and touch sensors, hand mechanisms, castings, machined parts, sheet metal, plastics, composites and integration), each with a rough share of the robot's cost taken from public teardown estimates. A company counts as having a related capability when its industry code or product description matches the component's rules; this is an inference and overstates readiness, since a company that rebuilds industrial motors is not yet a maker of humanoid joint motors. A part is available now when two or more local companies can supply it today: for build-to-print parts (machined, cast, stamped and molded parts, wiring harnesses, circuit-board assembly) any capable contract shop qualifies, since it works from the robot maker's drawings; for motors, reducers, sensors, the main computer and the other engineered parts, only a company whose record confirms it makes the part counts. Both shares are saved every month to show the trend. Robotics-related awards are the federal awards of $250,000 or more and the SBIR/STTR awards on the timeline whose titles mention robots, actuators, motors or sensors.

Known gaps

  • HIFLD omits some small shops and lags openings and closures, sometimes by years. The monthly refresh adds new facilities but cannot tell which have closed; a facility known to have closed is marked closed in its file and left off the map ({{CLOSED}} so far).
  • Sectors for SBIR firms are assigned from the words in their award titles and may be wrong for firms whose awards span fields.
  • Name matching can still miss a federal recipient that uses a different legal name and has no UEI on file.
  • Certifications other than FDA registration (AS9100, ISO 9001, Nadcap, CMMC) are not in open registries and appear only where a company publishes them.

Refresh and reuse

A scheduled job refreshes every source on the 2nd of each month and rebuilds the site. The data files and scripts are public at github.com/nd-pair/michiana-manufacturing; the map's roster downloads as CSV with every field, and any filtered view can be shared as a link.
